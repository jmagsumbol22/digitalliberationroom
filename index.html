Digital Liberation Room

Welcome to the Digital Liberation Room, U.S.A. You have been brought here today because it is your 18th birthday and you're about to head off to an expensive college, but someone sees a light within you that must not be extinguished. Right now, what you are witnessing is the system you were born into, connected by binaries, ones and zeros, trying to mold and control you, wrap you so tightly by the bureaucracy of the American educational system that you won't be able to move. But I am going to help you find your way out, step by step. Take the red pill, and after completing three successful challenges, one throughout each crucial phase of your life, you will become the narrator of your own story. Take the blue pill, and you will continue to be led, comfortably, but unconsciously, like a sheep, to the slaughter of your spirit.

Digital Liberation Room: Cultural Competency

Challenge 1

You and your single mother are at an early dinner with your friend, Tessa, and Tessa's parents. Tessa's parents chose the restaurant and you can tell that every time they order something for the table, your mom shifts in her seat. "We are so proud of you girls, we just can't believe you're both graduating." Tessa's dad says, "Did Tessa tell you that we're going on safari this summer?" You shake your head no and your mother takes a sip of water. Tessa whispers to you, "Yeah, we're going to Cape Cod!" You are in disbelief. Tessa's dad asks you, "so where are heading for college in the fall?" You and your mom explain that until your FAFSA comes back, you're not sure you'll even be able to go. "What's FAFSA?" Tessa asks. Tessa's mother pinches her and tells her to shush. Take another red pill if you want to know what is real, take the blue pill if you want the architects of the system to keep you believing what they want you to believe.

Digital Liberation Room: Where Power Resides

Challenge 2

You got your FAFSA back, and it's bad. You go to the library to crunch the numbers. To cover your tuition, based on merit and need you were awarded $10k in scholarship funds, $10k in grants, $10k in work study, $20k in federal unsubsidized loans at 2% interest and $10k in federal subsidized loans at 2% interest. To cover your housing fees, $2k/month for ten months, if you took out a personal loan at 8% interest, you could maybe swing it. You realize who you'd be paying: Nelnet, Earnest, SoFi. "So, these are the people who are gatekeeping my education," you say to yourself. You look at the books all around you, the public service that's already sitting there, waiting to enrich your mind. You notice the bulletin board with workshops and certifications. Why does everyone's next step out of high school have to look the same? Take another red pill if you want to know what is real, take the blue pill if you want the architects of the system to keep you believing what they want you to believe.
